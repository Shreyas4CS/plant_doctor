The AI project lifecycle

Six stages, each one unlocks the next

Every real AI product is built this way. Finish a stage and the next one opens.

STAGE 1

Problem scoping

Fill the 4Ws canvas: who has the problem, what it is, where it happens and why it matters.

STAGE 2

Data acquisition

Photograph leaves with the camera, sorted into Healthy, Yellowing and Spotted.

STAGE 3

Data exploration

See each photo the way a computer does: counts of green, yellow and brown pixels.

You decide the classes

Leaf classes you could create

Your team creates the classes, gives each one a name and a description, and adds as many as you need. Here are some ideas.

Class 1

Healthy

Even, rich green all over. No patches, no spots.

Mostly green pixelsSmooth colour
Class 2

Yellowing

Green fading to yellow, often from the edges or between the veins.

Many yellow pixelsWater or nutrient stress
Class 3

Spotted / Diseased

Brown or black spots and dead patches, sometimes with yellow rings.

Brown and dark pixelsFungus or bacteria
Class 4

Dried

Dry, brown and crispy. No disease, the leaf has just dried out.

Brown all overToo little water or heat

Good data tips

  • Use a plain white sheet behind the leaf. The AI should see the leaf, not the table.
  • Use bright, even light. Dark or yellow lamp light changes the colours the AI learns.
  • One leaf per photo, filling most of the box. Turn it a little between shots.
  • Keep the classes balanced. About the same number of photos in each class works best.

Stage 3 of 6

Data exploration Find the pattern

A computer cannot see "a sick leaf". It only sees thousands of tiny coloured dots called pixels. LeafLens sorts every pixel on the leaf into green, yellow or brown. Explore the charts below, then answer the questions to find the pattern your AI will use.

Colour bars: the average leaf in each class

Each bar shows how much of a leaf is green, yellow and brown. We added up all the photos in a class and found the average.

Green partYellow partBrown part

See what the computer sees

Tap any photo. On the right, every pixel of the leaf is painted green, yellow or brown, the way the computer sorted it. Grey is the background.

Dot chart: every photo is one dot

The further right a dot is, the more yellow that leaf has. The higher up a dot is, the more brown it has. A dot in the bottom-left corner is mostly green. Tap a dot to see its photo.

Why does this matter?

If each class has its own colours, a model can learn to tell them apart. Finding these patterns in the data is called exploring the data. The colours you find here become the clues for your models in Stage 4.

Stage 4 of 6

Modelling Two models

A model is the part of the AI that makes the decision. First, you choose how to split your photos into practice photos and secret test photos. Then you build Model A yourself and train Model B, using only the practice photos.

Step 1 · Choose how to split your photos

To check if an AI really learned, we test it on photos it has never seen. So we keep some photos aside before training.

Step 2 · Model A: the colour rule

A rule you make yourself, using the colours you found in Stage 3.

Step 3 · Model B: the neural network

A tiny computer brain that finds its own rule by studying your practice photos.

Stage 5 of 6

Evaluation Surprise test

Now both models take a surprise test on the secret photos they have never seen. The score shows how often each model was right. The mix-up table shows which classes it confused.

How it works

Your goal: build an AI that tells healthy, yellowing and spotted leaves apart. Each stage unlocks the next.

  1. Problem scopingFill the 4Ws and pick the AI domain.
  2. Data acquisitionCreate your own classes (name and description), then take at least 10 photos of each with the camera, upload photos, or add practice leaves.
  3. Data explorationExplore the colour bars and the dot chart, then answer the questions.
  4. ModellingChoose how to split your photos, set Model A's colour rule and train Model B.
  5. EvaluationRead the test accuracy and confusion matrix, then pick a model to deploy.
  6. DeploymentDiagnose 3 leaves with the Leaf Doctor to complete the project. Add wrong answers back to your data and retrain.

End this session?

The 4Ws, every photo, both models and the diagnosis log will be deleted from this device.

Project complete!

Your team took an AI from a problem statement all the way to a working Leaf Doctor. Keep testing leaves, add the ones it got wrong and retrain to make it better.
